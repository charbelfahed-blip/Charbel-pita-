KitchenAid → cold ferment → bake

A practical starting formula, not a kitchen-tested guarantee. Yeast amounts assume a 3–4°C fridge. Follow the dough’s expansion as well as the clock. Ball weights are before baking; unused reserved water slightly reduces yield.

1. Mix with your KitchenAid

For each mixer batch, combine both flours and the starting water by hand in the bowl. Cover and rest 25 minutes. Add yeast, salt and olive oil. If using active dry yeast, follow the packet’s rehydration directions using water from the recipe allowance.

Fit the dough hook. Knead on speed 2, checking after 2 minutes; about 4–6 minutes total may be enough. Stop when smooth, elastic and slightly tacky. Add reserved water gradually only if needed. Rest the dough 10 minutes if tight rather than forcing a long mix. Follow your model’s capacity and operating limits; stop if the mixer strains or gets unusually hot.

2. Refrigerate

Leave room for expansion. The dough should become aerated and puffy while remaining elastic. If nearly doubled early, bake sooner; if still dense after chilling, allow extra room-temperature time. Do not wait for it to collapse.

3. Relax and roll

Roll evenly to about 3 mm thick. A 45 g ball makes roughly a 14–15 cm pita; larger balls make wider rounds. If the dough springs back, cover and wait 10 minutes. Rest rolled discs covered for 10–15 minutes. Roll only a few ahead to prevent dry skins.

5. Keep soft and store

Move each baked pita under a clean towel immediately. Cool completely before sealing in bags. Freeze the surplus the same day in family-sized portions. Reheat briefly until flexible.

If a pita does not puff

Bake one test pita first. Check for an underheated baking surface, uneven rolling, tears or a dried surface. Pale and flat: allow more preheating. Burnt before puffing: reduce heat or move farther from the flame. Stiff after baking: shorten the bake and cover promptly. A flat pita still works as a wrap.
