KitchenAid → cold ferment → bake

A practical starting formula, not a kitchen-tested guarantee. Yeast amounts assume a 3–4°C fridge. Follow the dough’s expansion as well as the clock. Ball weights are before baking; unused reserved water slightly reduces yield.

1. Mix with your KitchenAid

For each mixer batch, combine both flours and the starting water by hand in the bowl. Cover and rest 25 minutes. Add yeast, salt and olive oil. If using active dry yeast, follow the packet’s rehydration directions using water from the recipe allowance.

Fit the dough hook. Knead on speed 2, checking after 2 minutes; about 4–6 minutes total may be enough. Stop when smooth, elastic and slightly tacky. Add reserved water gradually only if needed. Rest the dough 10 minutes if tight rather than forcing a long mix. Follow your model’s capacity and operating limits; stop if the mixer strains or gets unusually hot.

2. Refrigerate

Leave room for expansion. The dough should become aerated and puffy while remaining elastic. If nearly doubled early, bake sooner; if still dense after chilling, allow extra room-temperature time. Do not wait for it to collapse.

3. Relax and roll

Roll evenly to about 3 mm thick. A 45 g ball makes roughly a 14–15 cm pita; larger balls make wider rounds. If the dough springs back, cover and wait 10 minutes. Rest rolled discs covered for 10–15 minutes. Roll only a few ahead to prevent dry skins.

4. Choose your oven

5. Keep soft and store

Move each baked pita under a clean towel immediately. Cool completely before sealing in bags. Freeze the surplus the same day in family-sized portions. Reheat briefly until flexible.

If a pita does not puff

Bake one test pita first. Check for an underheated baking surface, uneven rolling, tears or a dried surface. Pale and flat: allow more preheating. Burnt before puffing: reduce heat or move farther from the flame. Stiff after baking: shorten the bake and cover promptly. A flat pita still works as a wrap.

Same-day Lebanese pita

Soft, flexible pita with a pocket · 65% hydration · 80% all-purpose flour + 20% whole wheat · easy to knead and roll by hand.

Plan on around 3–4 hours to reach the first bake, plus extra oven time for a 50-pita batch. No added sugar. All weights are raw dough weights.

Your same-day batch

IngredientWeight

Base quantities for 50 × 45 g: 1,060 g all-purpose, 265 g whole wheat, 861 g water, 9 g instant yeast, 23 g salt and 32 g olive oil. Total: 2,250 g. Quantities scale together; weighing to 0.1 g helps for small batches.

Step-by-step · manual method

1. Mix — about 5 minutes

Combine the all-purpose flour, whole wheat flour and instant yeast. Add water at around 28–30°C (82–86°F), mixing until no dry flour remains. The dough may look rough. The water should feel comfortably warm, never hot.

2. Covered rest — 20 minutes

Cover the dough and let the flour absorb the water. This makes the 20% whole-wheat dough easier to knead. Avoid adding extra flour at this stage.

3. Add salt and oil; knead — 8–12 minutes

Pinch and fold in all the salt and olive oil, then knead by hand on a clean, lightly oiled worktop. Push, fold and rotate until smooth and elastic, with a soft slightly tacky feel. If it fights back, cover and rest 5–10 minutes before continuing. For the default 2.25 kg batch, divide into two portions for easier handling; recombine for the rise.

4. First rise — about 60–90 minutes

Put the dough in lightly oiled, covered bowls at roughly 22–24°C (72–75°F). Wait until noticeably airy and around 70–100% larger, rather than following the clock blindly. A cooler kitchen may take longer; a warmer one may be quicker.

5. Divide and make balls — about 15–25 minutes

Divide into portions of your selected dough weight (45 g by default). Round smoothly with seams tucked underneath. Cover all the balls to prevent a dry skin.

6. Relax the balls — 20–30 minutes

Rest covered until the dough rolls without springing back. Begin preheating your oven or baking surface during this rest (earlier for a thick pizza stone).

7. Roll and short final rest — 10–15 minutes

On a lightly floured counter, roll each 45 g piece to roughly 12–14 cm across and 2–3 mm thick. Keep thickness even, including at the edges, and avoid holes. Larger or smaller dough weights need appropriately adjusted diameters. Keep rolled rounds covered for 10–15 minutes, rolling only a few ahead of the oven.

8. Bake — choose your equipment

9. Keep soft and store

Remove each pita as soon as it is inflated and cooked, before deep browning. Stack immediately under a clean towel; cool fully before sealing in bags. Freeze extras in portions the same day. Pitas that do not make pockets are still good as flatbread.

Tips for puffing and troubleshooting

No pocket: Check that the stone is properly preheated, the rounds are even without tears, and the surfaces have not dried out. Test one pita before baking all 50.

Dough springs back: Keep it covered and relax another 5–10 minutes.

Stiff after baking: Avoid adding too much bench flour and do not leave pitas in the oven until brown; cover them promptly.

Working through 50 pieces: Keep unbaked dough covered and stagger rolling/baking to prevent overproofing or drying.
